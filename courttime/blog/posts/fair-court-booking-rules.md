Every club with more players than courts runs into the same complaints. The same four people get the 6 p.m. slot every Tuesday. Somebody books three courts "just in case" and shows up for one. A member who wakes up early grabs the whole weekend.

None of those are bad people. They are doing what the system allows. If you want different behavior, change the rules. Better still, make the rules enforce themselves so nobody on the board has to play court police.

Here is a practical way to think about booking rules, starting with the ones that matter most.

## Start with the booking window

The **booking window** is how far ahead a member can reserve a court. It is the single most important rule you will set.

- **Too long** (two or three weeks) and planners lock up prime time before anyone else knows it is open.
- **Too short** (same day only) and people can't arrange a doubles match.

For most clubs, **3 to 7 days** is the sweet spot. Busy facilities with more demand than courts tend toward the short end.

One refinement that works well is a **release time**. Instead of slots opening at midnight, a day opens at a set time, say 7:00 a.m. seven days out. That puts everyone on equal footing instead of rewarding night owls.

## Cap how much one member can hold

A booking window controls *when* people can book. Limits control *how much*. The three most useful are:

1. **Max active reservations**: how many upcoming bookings a member can hold at once. Two is a common starting point. When one is played, they can book another.
2. **Max duration**: how long a single booking can be. 90 minutes covers most singles and doubles play.
3. **Max hours per week**: a backstop for the most enthusiastic members.

Also consider **no overlapping reservations**, so a member can't hold two courts at the same time, and a **minimum lead time**, so nobody books a court five minutes before it starts while someone is walking up to it.

## Treat peak hours differently

Weekday evenings and weekend mornings are where conflicts actually happen. Off-peak courts at 1 p.m. on a Wednesday rarely need limits at all.

So instead of tightening rules everywhere, set **peak hours** and give them their own caps. For example, allow one peak-hour booking per week and a 60-minute maximum during peak times. A separate **weekend policy** does the same for Saturday and Sunday.

This keeps your rules permissive when courts are empty and strict only when it matters. Members notice the difference.

## Think in households, not just individuals

If your club or HOA has family memberships, individual limits have a loophole. A family of four can hold four times as many courts as a single member.

**Household rules** close that gap:

- A cap on active reservations **per household**, not just per person
- A household cap on **peak-hour** bookings
- A limit on how many accounts can be tied to **one address**, which is especially useful for HOAs where membership comes with the home

## Make no-shows cost something

The most frustrating booking is the one nobody uses. A court sits empty while someone else wanted it.

A **strike system** handles this without awkward conversations. A no-show or a late cancellation earns a strike. Too many strikes inside a set window, for example three in 30 days, means a short lockout from booking, perhaps 7 days.

The point is not punishment. Once members know the rule exists and is applied evenly, they cancel early and the problem mostly goes away.

## Reserve courts for the club's own programs

Lessons, clinics, leagues, and maintenance all need court time. Rather than booking these by hand as fake member reservations, use:

- **Blackouts** for maintenance windows and events
- **Admin-only courts** that members can see but only staff can book
- **Walk-up courts** that stay off the online system entirely, if your community wants some first-come, first-served play

## Write the rules down where members will see them

The rules should be visible at the moment of booking, not buried in a PDF from 2019. Members should see a clear message when a booking breaks a rule, for example "You already have 2 upcoming reservations." They shouldn't hit a vague error or an angry email from the board.

It also helps to have members **accept your general rules and court waiver** when they sign up. Then everyone has agreed to the same thing.

## A starter rule set

If you are starting from nothing, this is a reasonable default for a busy 4–8 court club:

| Rule | Starting value |
|---|---|
| Booking window | 7 days, opening at 7:00 a.m. |
| Max active reservations | 2 per member, 3 per household |
| Max duration | 90 minutes |
| Peak hours | Weekdays 5–8 p.m., weekends 8 a.m.–noon |
| Peak-hour cap | 1 per member per week, 60 minutes max |
| Strikes | 3 no-shows or late cancels in 30 days = 7-day lockout |

Run it for a month, look at who is booking and when, and adjust. Rules that change based on real usage get much less pushback than rules that change based on whoever complained last.
